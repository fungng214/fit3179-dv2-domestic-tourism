For readers in Malaysia: domestic tourism means Australian residents travelling within Australia. These are trips, not unique people; one person may travel several times. A June quarter covers April to June, not a whole year. Money is shown in AUD (A$). Hover over chart marks or map labels to see values, dates and units. Use the trip-type filter on Figure 6 to compare overnight and day trips.

01

A recovery with a bigger price tag

Australian residents made 27.1 million overnight trips in April–June 2026. Trip numbers were close to their 2019 level, but the money spent was much higher. A common baseline makes that difference visible.

Read the timeline carefully. The Domestic Tourism Statistics collection (DoTS) replaced the National Visitor Survey in January 2025. The 2019–2024 values here use TRA’s revised backcast for closer comparability. They are estimates reconstructed for the new collection, not the original NVS figures. The series transition is marked on the chart; revised estimates do not remove all uncertainty.

Figure 01 · Indexed line chart

Spending has grown much faster than trip numbers

By June quarter 2026, nominal overnight spending was 66.1% above 2019, while trips were only 4.2% higher. The spending line includes price changes; it is not a measure of real growth.

Destinations have recovered at different speeds

Tasmania and Western Australia recorded about 15% more visits than in June quarter 2019. The ACT remained 7.6% below its 2019 level. These estimates describe change, not statistical significance.

03

Holidays grow as business travel falls

Why people travel matters as much as how many trips they take. The latest comparable June quarters show holidays and business moving in different directions. Most overnight trips still stay within a state.

Figure 06 · Grouped bar chart

Holiday and business travel moved in opposite directions

Overnight trips reporting holidays rose from 12.11 million to 12.95 million. Those reporting business fell from 3.25 million to 2.02 million. Use the dropdown to compare day trips too.

Australia’s domestic tourism story is about more than recovery in trip counts. The purpose of travel, the destination and the money spent reveal different kinds of change.

Sources and how to read the data

The statistical series

Tourism Research Australia (TRA) supplies domestic trips, nights and expenditure. The latest two-year comparisons use the June quarter 2026 DoTS workbook. Historical comparisons use only June quarters from the revised 2019–2024 overnight backcast, joined to the 2025–2026 DoTS estimates. The new collection covers Australian residents aged 14 years and over and changed collection methods and routine travel treatment. Read TRA’s explanation of the change.

Four definitions that matter

  • Trips and destination visits: national trips count journeys. State totals count visits to destinations; a multi-state journey can appear in more than one state. State visit totals are therefore not shares that must add to the national trip total.
  • Visit reason: the purpose chart reports reasons recorded during a trip. Reasons can overlap. It compares counts rather than constructing a 100% breakdown. Expenditure by main trip purpose uses a different definition and is not divided by these counts.
  • Visitor nights per resident: June quarter 2026 visitor nights divided by the ABS estimated resident population at 31 March 2026. The denominator covers all ages and approximates the population at the start of the quarter. It describes tourism activity relative to destination population, not local residents’ propensity to travel.
  • Money and uncertainty: spending is in nominal Australian dollars. Changes include price effects and are not inflation-adjusted. Values are survey and model estimates; displayed precision supports traceability rather than claims of exact counts or statistically significant differences.
