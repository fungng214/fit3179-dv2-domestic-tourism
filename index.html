01

A recovery with a bigger price tag

Australian residents made 27.1 million overnight trips in April–June 2026.
Trip numbers were close to their 2019 level, but the money spent
was much higher.

Sources and how to read the data

The statistical series

Tourism Research Australia (TRA) supplies domestic trips, nights and expenditure.
The latest two-year comparisons use the June quarter 2026 DoTS workbook.
Historical comparisons use only June quarters from the revised 2019–2024 overnight backcast,
joined to the 2025–2026 DoTS estimates.
The new collection covers Australian residents aged 14 years and over and
changed collection methods and routine travel treatment.
Read TRA’s explanation of the change.

Four definitions that matter

  • Trips and destination visits: national trips count journeys.
    State totals count visits to destinations; a multi-state journey can appear in
    more than one state.
    State visit totals are therefore not shares that must add to the
    national trip total.
  • Visit reason: the purpose chart reports reasons recorded during a trip.
    Reasons can overlap.
    It compares counts rather than constructing a 100% breakdown.
    Expenditure by main trip purpose uses a different definition and is not
    divided by these counts.
  • Visitor nights per resident: June quarter 2026 visitor nights divided by the
    ABS estimated resident population at 31 March 2026.
    The denominator covers all ages and approximates the population at the start
    of the quarter.
    It describes tourism activity relative to destination population, not local residents’ propensity
    to travel.
  • Money and uncertainty: spending is in nominal Australian dollars.
    Changes include price effects and are not inflation-adjusted.
    Values are survey and model estimates; displayed precision supports traceability rather than
    claims of exact counts or statistically significant differences.
